# Volume II evidence and adaptation register

This register separates details represented as Ireland's fiction from spatial and interactive additions made for **The Holy Office**. It is a production aid, not a critical edition. Chapter references follow the 1834 second edition linked from the application's About panel; the local source available to this project is OCR-derived and wording should be checked against page images before any future direct quotation is added.

The second plan of the building (the replan recorded in `implementation-notes.md` under *Stage D*) was made without fresh access to the source text — the network policy of the session that made it did not reach the digitised volume — so every reading below that was not already in this register is marked **(from memory)** and should be checked against the page images before it is relied on.

## Player identity

The player is the unnamed, cloaked guide who conducts Duca Bertocci out of the dungeons. The fiction gives him a habit resembling that of the officials, knowledge of the underground route, the required password, and the key to the final door. He reveals that he once intended to kill Bertocci but has now saved him. The game **infers** from this episode that the habit permits broad, unnoticed circulation. Unlimited access is a play premise, not a literal claim that the novel demonstrates access to every room.

## Adaptation rules

1. The dungeon is an imagined literary reconstruction, not a historical plan of a Florentine Inquisition building.
2. Direct quotations are avoided in the current interface. Literary material is closely paraphrased and paired with interpretation.
3. Concealed cell links, service stairs, loops, patrol routes, and the player's universal access are declared interactive inventions.
4. Violence is conveyed through testimony, sound, absence, and threatened procedure rather than spectacle.
5. NPC dialogue is original period-inflected adaptation text. It must not be presented as Ireland's wording.
6. The one lock in the building that answers to a key rather than to the habit is the lock on the way out, because that is the one the fiction gives the guide a key for.
