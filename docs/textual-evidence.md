# Volume II evidence and adaptation register

This register separates details represented as Ireland's fiction from spatial and interactive additions made for **The Holy Office**. It is a production aid, not a critical edition. Chapter references follow the 1834 second edition linked from the application's About panel; the local source available to this project is OCR-derived and wording should be checked against page images before any future direct quotation is added.

## Player identity

The player is the unnamed, cloaked guide who conducts Duca Bertocci out of the dungeons. The fiction gives him a habit resembling that of the officials, knowledge of the underground route, the required password, and the key to the final door. He reveals that he once intended to kill Bertocci but has now saved him. The game **infers** from this episode that the habit permits broad, unnoticed circulation. Unlimited access is a play premise, not a literal claim that the novel demonstrates access to every room.

## Evidence by sequence

| Sequence | Details grounded in the romance | Interactive interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admission | Closed vehicles, separated captives, guarded admission, denial of prisoners' presence | Gate court and office share a controlled threshold; officials patrol between them. |
| High passages | Lofty passages, lamps separated by darkness, disembodied steps, groans and cries | Lighting islands, sound motifs, and branching corridors make hearing precede seeing. |
| Tribunal | Black-covered table, low seat for the accused, officials, secretary, crucifix, repeated crosses, formal recording and coercion | A central administrative chamber with service circulation around its perimeter. |
| Marcello's cell | Sparse furniture, weak lamp, infernal mural, Bendetta Cazzala's damaged record, apparent supernatural movement, concealed theatrical mechanism | The guide recognizes the mechanism behind a terror that remains opaque to the prisoner. |
| Maddalena's cell | Plainer confinement, spider/web, small gold cross, imaginative transformation of lamp, wall, and damp air | Her journal layer emphasizes interior freedom rather than treating the cell as another puzzle. |
| Escape | Disguising garment, masked checkpoint, omitted password, hidden route, locked moonlit exit | Wardrobe, password vault, service stair, and moon door form a navigable escape spine. |
| Threatened torture | Threats, cries through walls, recollection, and fearful anticipation carry more force than direct spectacle | The adjoining room remains unoccupied and non-graphic; sound, grille, cords, and inert apparatus imply institutional violence. |

## Adaptation rules

1. The dungeon is an imagined literary reconstruction, not a historical plan of a Florentine Inquisition building.
2. Direct quotations are avoided in the current interface. Literary material is closely paraphrased and paired with interpretation.
3. Concealed cell links, service stairs, loops, patrol routes, and the player's universal access are declared interactive inventions.
4. Violence is conveyed through testimony, sound, absence, and threatened procedure rather than spectacle.
5. NPC dialogue is original period-inflected adaptation text. It must not be presented as Ireland's wording.
